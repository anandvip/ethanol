In one paragraph. On 23 October 2026 the Centre amended the CMVR to allow E85 and E100 fuel in vehicles built and certified as flex-fuel; it did not order any change at petrol pumps, and the Petroleum Minister said on 27 October there is no plan to go beyond E20. The Supreme Court has not ruled against ethanol or any feedstock; on 29 October 2026 a bench asked the Centre how supply for 100%-ethanol vehicles would be arranged, in a case about ethanol allocation. The sharper shift on feedstock came from the executive: for ESY 2026-27 the Government set sugar diversion to ethanol at zero, and the OMC tender left out cane juice, sugar syrup and B-heavy molasses. 1. How farmers reach the schemes, state by state Grokcos

No central scheme pays farmers for ethanol as such. Farmers gain through the cane price that mills must pay, through state support to move land into maize, and through central schemes that run through each state's agriculture department. This section lists the routes and the official pages a farmer or officer can open. Contacts for the people named are in section 6.

3. Feedstocks: sugarcane, maize and grain Grokcos

Sugarcane Grokcos

  • Production: a record 500.063 million tonnes in 2025-26 (third advance estimates), against 454.611 Mt in 2024-25 — PIB 2265965, 27 May 2026.
  • Price: FRP ₹365 per quintal for 2026-27 (section 1). Cane dues: 99.5% paid for 2024-25; 88.6% for 2025-26 as of 20 Apr 2026 — PIB 2258113.
  • Ethanol routes from cane: cane juice or sugar syrup, B-heavy molasses and C-heavy molasses. About 40 LMT of sugar went to ethanol in 2024-25 (PIB 2155110). For ESY 2026-27 the Government set diversion to zero, which leaves only C-heavy molasses (below).

Maize Grokcos

  • Production: a record 55.093 Mt in 2025-26, up 11.684 Mt from 43.409 Mt — PIB 2265965.
  • Share in ethanol: from about 0% in ESY 2021-22 to about 42% of supply, as stated by the Petroleum Minister — PIB 2154355.
  • Programmes: maize cluster development and the IIMR catchment projects (section 1).

4. Process, plants, investment and policy Grokcos

Process Grokcos

1G from cane: juice or molasses → fermentation → distillation → dehydration to anhydrous (fuel-grade) ethanol. 1G from grain (maize, rice): milling → liquefaction and saccharification → fermentation → distillation and dehydration, with DDGS as an animal-feed co-product. 2G: crop residue and other lignocellulose, supported under PM JI-VAN.

BackgroundThe process description above is general background; it is not drawn from one government document. Grokcos

Plants and supply chain Grokcos

  • Capacity: from 421 crore litres (2014) to about 2,000 crore litres (2026), per the PIB research document of 5 Jul 2026 (PIB document, PDF).
  • 2G plants: IOCL Panipat (dedicated 10 Aug 2022); BPCL Bargarh, HPCL Bathinda and NRL Numaligarh were described as nearing completion in Aug 2024 (PIB 2043926; PIB 2154355).
  • Supply chain: OMC issue tenders each ESY (Nov–Oct) and allocate volumes; LTOA with DEP underpin grain capacity (PIB 2155110). Allocation disputes are now before the Supreme Court (section 2).
Government estimatesThe same PIB document reports, for ESY 2014-15 to May 2026: foreign-exchange savings of ₹1.90 lakh crore or more, additional payments to farmers of ₹1.60 lakh crore or more, 310+ LMT of crude substituted and about 930 LMT of CO₂ avoided. These are the Government's own estimates. Grokcos
